Research study

Before you begin

You are invited to take part in a short research study about how people evaluate everyday consumer products. Please read this page before deciding whether to take part.

  • What you will do. You will read a short article about a free display tool, choose between two such tools and answer a few questions. The study takes about 2–3 minutes.
  • A display tool at the end. At the end, the tool you choose runs full-screen in this browser tab for about 5 seconds: slow, dim color sweeps, or a dark screen with a slow, dim wave. Neither flashes. Nothing is installed and no settings are changed. You can stop it at any time with Esc or the Stop button.
  • Please do not take part if you have photosensitive epilepsy.
  • Voluntary participation. Taking part is entirely voluntary. You may stop at any time by closing this window, without giving a reason and without penalty. Answers you give before leaving the study are kept unless you ask us to remove them.
  • Anonymity. Your responses are anonymous. We do not collect your name, email address, or any other directly identifying information.
  • Use of the data. Responses are stored securely and analysed only in aggregate for academic research purposes.
  • Risks and benefits. There are no anticipated risks beyond those of ordinary daily life. There is no direct benefit to you other than any compensation stated in the recruitment advertisement.
  • Questions. If you have questions about this study, you may contact the research team using the details provided where you found this study.

    A note on what you saw. This study looks at how people respond to product tests that agree or disagree; some participants saw such tests and others did not. TintCycle, BlankRest, their developers, and any magazines or product tests you saw were invented for this study, and no test was run. The display you just saw was made by the research team and does nothing to your screen. There is no independent evidence that any short screen tool, including real apps that make similar claims, reduces or prevents image retention. On most modern monitors image retention is rare and fades by itself; manufacturers recommend a screen saver, letting the display sleep, and varying what is on screen.
